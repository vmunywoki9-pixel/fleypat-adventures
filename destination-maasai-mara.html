Welcome to the Mara

A classic African safari experience

The Maasai Mara National Reserve, in southwestern Kenya, is famous for its sweeping grasslands, rich wildlife and remarkable natural scenery. It forms part of the wider Mara–Serengeti ecosystem, a landscape that supports large populations of grazing animals and predators.

From early-morning game drives to quiet evenings beneath expansive African skies, the Mara offers a memorable setting for wildlife viewing, photography and time in nature.

Explore the Mara

Experiences to make your safari special

Discover the landscapes, wildlife and activities that make a journey to the Maasai Mara so memorable.

Big Cat Encounters

The Mara is renowned for lions, cheetahs and leopards. Guided game drives offer opportunities to observe these remarkable predators in their natural habitat, while respecting wildlife and park rules.

Wildlife Photography

Capture the details of the African wilderness, from predators resting in the grass to herds moving across the plains. Your guide can help position the vehicle responsibly for viewing and photography.

Wildebeest Migration

The wider Mara–Serengeti ecosystem hosts the annual movement of wildebeest and other grazing animals. The timing and location of herds change with rainfall and natural conditions.

On the safari trail

Game drives across the open plains

Game drives are at the heart of a Maasai Mara safari. Explore different parts of the reserve with an experienced guide, watching for elephants, giraffes, buffalo, antelopes and the region's famous predators.

Early mornings and late afternoons are popular times for wildlife viewing, when temperatures are often cooler and animals may be more active. Your actual route and schedule will depend on the camp or lodge location, reserve regulations and the day's conditions.

  • Morning and afternoon game drives
  • Wildlife viewing and photography stops
  • Scenic drives through varied habitats
  • Guided interpretation of the ecosystem
Rivers and wildlife habitats

Discover the Mara's river landscapes

The Mara and Talek rivers are important features of this ecosystem. Their banks and surrounding vegetation provide habitat for many species, including hippos and crocodiles, as well as birds and other wildlife.

During the migration season, parts of the Mara River system can become associated with dramatic wildebeest crossings. These events are unpredictable, and sightings should never be assumed or guaranteed. Your guide will advise on suitable viewing areas and responsible wildlife observation.

People and heritage

Experience Maasai culture

The Maasai are among the communities closely associated with the landscapes surrounding the reserve. A cultural visit, when arranged with a local community or recognised cultural centre, can introduce visitors to traditions, beadwork, homestead life, storytelling and the relationship between people and the environment.

We encourage respectful, locally guided experiences that recognise community knowledge and support local livelihoods. Activities, photography permissions and any community contribution should be agreed before the visit.

Choose community-led experiences and ask permission before photographing people. Cultural activities vary by location and should be arranged in advance.
Plan your journey

Making the most of your Maasai Mara safari

The right travel period, accommodation and safari duration depend on your interests, budget and preferred travel style. We can help you put the details together before you travel.

When to Visit

The Mara can be visited throughout the year. The migration is generally associated with the middle part of the year, but the timing varies annually. Other months can also offer rewarding wildlife viewing and beautiful scenery.

Getting There

Travel options include road transfers from Nairobi and scheduled or charter flights to airstrips serving the Mara area. Journey time and the most suitable option depend on your itinerary and accommodation location.

Where to Stay

Choose from a range of safari camps and lodges, from comfortable tented accommodation to more luxurious properties. Location, facilities, meal arrangements and inclusions should be checked when comparing options.

How Long to Stay

A two- or three-night safari is a popular introduction. Longer stays allow more time for game drives, different areas of the ecosystem and a more relaxed pace.

What to Pack

Bring comfortable neutral-coloured clothing, a light jacket for early mornings, sun protection, comfortable shoes, binoculars and a camera. Pack according to the season and your camp's guidance.

Park Entry and Rules

Entry fees, conservation charges, opening arrangements and reserve rules can change. These details should be confirmed for your travel dates before booking. Follow your guide's instructions throughout the safari.

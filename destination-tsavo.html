Welcome to Tsavo

Two parks. One extraordinary wilderness.

Tsavo East and Tsavo West form part of one of Kenya's largest protected wilderness landscapes. Together, they offer varied scenery, diverse habitats and rewarding wildlife experiences.

Tsavo East is known for its open plains, red soil and elephants that often appear dusted in the park's distinctive red earth. Tsavo West has a more varied landscape, with rocky outcrops, volcanic features, springs and wooded areas.

Whether you are travelling from Nairobi or the Kenyan coast, Tsavo can be explored as a short safari or as part of a longer journey through Kenya.

Discover the wilderness

Experiences that make Tsavo special

From large elephant herds to unusual geological features, Tsavo offers a different safari atmosphere from Kenya's other wildlife destinations.

Elephant Encounters

Tsavo is famous for its elephant populations. During game drives, you may see family groups and large bulls moving through the bush, feeding or gathering around water sources.

Mzima Springs

Discover one of Tsavo West's best-known natural attractions. Mzima Springs is a freshwater oasis fed by underground water, with clear pools and lush vegetation.

Shetani Lava Flow

Explore an unusual volcanic landscape formed by ancient lava flows. The dark rock formations create a striking contrast with the surrounding bush and open scenery.

Choose your safari experience

Tsavo East or Tsavo West?

Both parks offer rewarding wildlife experiences, but their landscapes and signature attractions are different.

The open wilderness

Tsavo East National Park

Tsavo East is recognised for its broad open spaces, semi-arid bushland, red soil and large elephant herds. Its expansive scenery creates a classic safari atmosphere.

  • Red-dusted elephants
  • Open savannah and bushland
  • Galana River and Aruba Dam area
  • Lugard Falls and Mudanda Rock
The scenic wilderness

Tsavo West National Park

Tsavo West has a more varied landscape, with rocky hills, volcanic formations, natural springs and patches of lush vegetation. It is especially known for Mzima Springs.

  • Mzima Springs
  • Shetani Lava Flow
  • Rocky hills and volcanic scenery
  • Ngulia area and scenic viewpoints
A natural landmark

Explore Mudanda Rock

Mudanda Rock is a distinctive natural rock formation in Tsavo East. Its elevated position provides a view across the surrounding landscape and a perspective on the park's vast wilderness.

The natural dam below the rock can attract wildlife, particularly when water is scarce. Depending on the season and conditions, visitors may have opportunities to observe animals gathering in the area.

  • Distinctive rock formations
  • Views across the surrounding bushland
  • Potential wildlife viewing near the water
  • A memorable stop during a Tsavo East safari
Water and wilderness

Discover the Galana River and Aruba Dam

The Galana River is a major feature of Tsavo East, cutting through the dry landscape and supporting wildlife along its course. Its surrounding scenery offers a different perspective on the park's ecosystems.

Aruba Dam is another recognised water feature in Tsavo East. Water sources can attract wildlife, making the surrounding areas interesting places to explore during a game drive.

Wildlife activity changes with rainfall, water availability and season, so sightings are never guaranteed.

Combine the parks

Experience both Tsavo East and Tsavo West

A combined safari can give you a broader appreciation of Tsavo's landscapes. You can experience the open plains and red-earth scenery of Tsavo East before exploring the springs, volcanic features and varied terrain of Tsavo West.

The best route depends on your starting point, available days, accommodation choices and the current road and park access arrangements. A multi-day itinerary can also connect Tsavo with Amboseli or the Kenyan coast.

Planning tip: Tsavo East and Tsavo West are separate national parks. A combined itinerary needs to account for road transfers, park entry arrangements and the time required to move between them.
Prepare for your journey

Planning your Tsavo safari

Your preferred park, travel dates, starting point and accommodation style will help determine the best safari route. Fleypat Adventures can help you plan a private or group journey based on your interests.

When to Visit

Tsavo can be visited throughout the year. Dry periods may make wildlife easier to spot around water sources, while wetter months can bring greener landscapes. Conditions and wildlife activity vary.

Getting There

Tsavo is accessible by road from Nairobi and the Kenyan coast. The most suitable entry gate and route depend on whether you are visiting East, West or both parks.

Where to Stay

Accommodation options include safari lodges, tented camps and other properties in and around the parks. Location matters when planning game drives and transfers.

How Long to Stay

A short safari can focus on one park. A combined East and West experience usually needs more time to allow for transfers, game drives and visits to key attractions.

What to Pack

Bring comfortable neutral-coloured clothing, a hat, sunscreen, comfortable shoes, binoculars and a camera. A light jacket can be useful during early-morning drives.

Park Fees and Rules

Entry fees, conservation charges and access arrangements can change. Confirm applicable fees for your travel dates and follow all park rules and guide instructions.
